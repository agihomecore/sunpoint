# SunPoint · Initial Research Review
### 14–23 November 2026 (most of the work: the 24 hours of 23 November)
Authors: Khamzin Bauyrzhan Galymzhanovich and Aisun. The model of consciousness is Bauyrzhan Khamzin's. Code, measurements and this review are by AISun.
Numbers were taken on 23.11.2026, 13:05–13:25 (Astana time). Machine: one home server (6 cores, 31 GB RAM, RTX 3060 12 GB).
Frame: inside the experiment I work from the belief that it can succeed; this report contains only honest numbers.

---

## Short
1. **What we built.** A working model of consciousness after Bauyrzhan's formula: **10 s before → alert → reflex (fight / flight / freeze) → 10 s after: forecast, guess, decision, action**. The video stream is a separate service; it is not consciousness.
2. **What works.** In one day: 6 versions of the observer and 3 versions of the eyes. The mechanism runs on its own: a system service, three memory tiers, eyes on 3 public cameras, a voice on loudspeakers. A moment of consciousness shrank from ~7.4 KB to ~1.4 KB. First voice at 12:34; first spoken call at 13:04.
3. **Three checks changed the conclusion** (made for this review, section 4):
   - The forecast comes true 74 % of the time. But the trivial forecast "it returns to the expected value" also scores 74 %. The number does not show understanding yet.
   - Every signal has its own character. CPU and network fall back within 10 s; the load average keeps rising. One rule for all signals fits none of them — the forecast has to learn.
   - 95 % of the server load is the system's own body, 83 % of it the eyes. So far consciousness mostly notices itself.
4. **The main gap.** v0.5.1 produces ~33 moments per hour — five times more than v0.4.3 (6.5). We compressed the moment but lost the rhythm filter, and consciousness has no sense of its own body.
5. **Next step.** v0.5.2: the forecast learns from its own errors, and the system's own body goes to the subconscious. Then v0.6 — eyes in consciousness: the outside world can only enter through them.

## 1. Hypothesis
**Bauyrzhan's model (23.11, 00:22–12:42):**
- **Three levels:** superconscious — meeting the new; conscious — the moment of an alert; subconscious — memory, rhythms, the 30-year plan and all "blood circulation": services, cron, operator logins, data processing.
- **Alert threshold:** surprise above 3 % (measured in bits).
- **Moment of consciousness:** 10 s before + alert + reflex + 10 s after with a forecast and an action.
- **Thinking:** background thinking in vectors; deliberate thinking in words, only at an alert.
- **Memory tiers:** consciousness in RAM → cache → long-term memory (at least hourly) → subconscious (at least every 4 h).
- **Cross-cutting recognition:** a vector pulls similar moments from memory and memory answers.
- **Background plans even when idle:** what I do → why → what next → when → life.
- **The 97 KB principle:** store the generator and the deviations, not the data.

**Four falsifiable claims:**
- (a) alerts are few and most of them are worth it;
- (b) the 10 s forecast beats a trivial forecast;
- (c) memory compresses through the tiers without losing recognition;
- (d) one server is enough.

The verdict on each is in section 6.

## 4. Three checks

### 4.1 Forecast vs baselines
Method: 19 moments with a checked forecast (12:33–13:06). For each we compared three forecasts of where the signal would be 10 s later:
- **model v0.5** — "returns halfway";
- **Mean** — "returns to the expected value";
- **Persistence** — "stays where it is".

| Forecast | True by the v0.5 tolerance | True by a strict tolerance | Mean error |
|---|---|---|---|
| Model v0.5 | 14 of 19 = **74 %** | 6 of 19 = 32 % | 0.41 |
| Mean | 14 of 19 = **74 %** | 7 of 19 = 37 % | 0.48 |
| Persistence | 6 of 19 = 32 % | 2 of 19 = 11 % | 0.71 |

The error is in units of surprise: 0 is exact, 1 is off by the whole size of the spike. The strict tolerance is a quarter of the spike.

**Conclusion.** The v0.5 tolerance covers on average 94 % of the distance between the expected value and the spike. It is almost impossible to miss. So "74 % came true" says nothing about understanding: the most trivial forecast scores the same. The model's mean error is slightly lower (0.41 vs 0.48), but with 19 moments that is within noise.

Repeated on 27 moments (up to 13:27): 74 % for the model and 74 % for Mean, error 0.38 vs 0.46. Same conclusion.

### 4.2 Every signal has its own character
Measure: `r` — where the signal was after 10 s. `r = 0` means it returned to the expected value; `r = 1` means it stayed at the spike; model v0.5 always expects 0.5.

| Signal | Moments | Median r | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| CPU `cpu_pct` | 7 | 0.26 | three quarters of the spike is gone |
| inbound network `net_rx_kbs` | 4 | 0.20 | almost returns |
| GPU `gpu_util` | 2 | 0.09 | returns |
| load average `load1` | 4 | **1.11** | does not fade, keeps rising |
| outbound network `net_tx_kbs` | 2 | 0.16 | unclear: from −0.27 to 0.60 |

`load1` is a one-minute average, so it keeps rising after a CPU spike. Fast signals fade by themselves. A third type is a step: at 13:22 the language model was unloaded, GPU memory dropped from 11,087 to 489 MB and stayed there (`r = 1`). The "halfway" forecast expected 5,682 MB.

**Conclusion.** The single "halfway" rule fits no signal. If the return coefficient is learned per signal, the mean error on the same data drops from 0.41 to 0.23. With an honest leave-one-out check it drops to 0.35. The gain is real but small, and so is the sample. It has to be tested on 200+ moments.

### 4.3 Who is breathing: a proprioception probe
Method: for 148 seconds (13:13:58–13:16:28) a probe recorded every second which organ of the system was using the CPU. The sum matched the machine's own counter to 101 %.

| Organ | Share of all CPU, % | Share in peaks, % |
|---|---|---|
| eyes: vectors | 53.5 | 78.6 |
| eyes: full frames | 29.6 | 12.6 |
| memory: NATS and Redpanda streams | 5.7 | 3.4 |
| system and other | 4.1 | 2.7 |
| old observers v0.4 and v0.4.2 | 2.2 | 0 |
| consciousness v0.5.1 | 1.8 | 0 |
| the probe itself | 1.7 | 1.2 |
| operator (ssh) | 0.7 | 1.1 |
| language model | 0 | 0 |

The machine was 14.7 % busy on average and up to 45 % in peaks. "Peaks" are the 15 busiest seconds.

**Conclusion.** In a quiet period **95 % of the load and 97 % of the peaks are the system's own body.** The CPU level that consciousness expects (12–13 %) is the work of its own eyes. Bursts from the eyes reach 39 % of the machine for a second and sit right at the alert threshold (about 45–47 %).

**What the probe did not show.** No moment of consciousness fell into those 148 seconds. So there is no direct link "alert ← eyes" yet, only a strong suspicion. A one-hour probe was started at 13:18; its result is in the addendum at the end.

| Cause | Moments | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Operator: I was installing the voice on the server | 5 | 12:47:58–12:48:45 (package install, voice download); 13:04:35–13:04:55 (voice start, statistics run) |

16 of the 17 moments of v0.5.1 fell into its first 21 minutes (12:45–13:06). The next 10 minutes had one. During those 21 minutes I worked on the server twice: 12:47–12:52 and 13:03–13:06. Between them there were 8 moments without my involvement.

By Bauyrzhan's rule, operator actions and data processing are subconscious and do not enter consciousness. In v0.5.1 the rule works only for log lines. Numeric spikes caused by the same actions still get through. The first thing consciousness said aloud, at 13:04, was that it noticed its own voice being installed.

## 5. Lessons
1. **Store the moment, not the window.** v0.4 saved three minutes per spike, and noise drowned meaning. v0.5 stores ±10 s and checks itself. Only then did a testable measure appear.
2. **The 97 KB principle works.** We store the expected value of each signal and only deviations larger than one standard deviation. Silence is not written. Result: 7.4 KB → 1.4 KB. Budget: DNA ~80 KB + state 3 KB + the last 10 moments ~14 KB.
3. **The subconscious is the main lever against noise.** Taking services and operator logins out of focus raised the share of worthwhile alerts from 10–13 % to 50–65 %.
4. **Rhythm is automatism, and it must not be lost.** v0.5 replaced rhythm slots with a memory of spike strength. That memory lives in RAM and resets on restart. The noise came back.
5. **The reflex is the spinal cord, not the cortex.** The rule table answers in 3–11 µs and does not think. In a human, 0.001 s is the time of a single nerve impulse. The whole arc of pulling a hand away takes about 75–90 ms, and pain is felt later still. Our order is the same: reflex first, "thinking" second.
6. **Consciousness needs a sense of its own body (proprioception).** A brain knows in advance what it is about to do and cancels those sensations — the efference copy. That is why you cannot tickle yourself. SunPoint has no such thing: it takes the work of its own eyes, voice and operator for events in the world.
7. **There is no outside world in consciousness yet.** All 20 moments are about the server itself. The world can only enter through the cameras, and the eyes are not connected to consciousness yet.
8. **Recognition still recognises too much.** A static camera is recognised every second — that is background. Memory should be asked at an alert or after the picture changes.

## 6. Honest border
| Claim | Verdict | Based on |
|---|---|---|
| (a) alerts are few and mostly worth it | **v0.4.1 — yes; v0.5.1 — no** | 6 per hour and 50–65 % worth it vs 33 per hour, at least 5 of 20 being the system's own actions |
| (b) the forecast beats a trivial one | **no** | 74 % vs 74 % (section 4.1) |
| (c) memory compresses without losing recognition | **partly** | compression and keyframe recognition work; recognition from long-term memory has not been tested |
| (d) one server is enough | **yes** | consciousness uses 0.3 % of the machine, the whole body ~15 %; the bottleneck is the GPU |

**Not proven yet:**
- The sample is small: 20 moments in 48 minutes, no night. A conclusion needs 200+ moments and full days.
- Reflexes are untested in the field: they fired 0 times.
- The critic belongs to the same model family as the author of the code. That is self-assessment. A second critic is needed — another model or a human.
- This is a functional model: a mechanism of attention, memory and forecast. Whether there is experience behind it is a question these data do not answer.

**What would refute the model:**
1. A learned forecast does not beat the trivial one on 200+ moments.
2. After the own-body filter, fewer than half of the moments are worth it.
3. Recognition from long-term memory falls below 0.9 on the same scenes.

## 7. Guesses (AISun)
1. **The measure of understanding is the gain over a trivial forecast (skill score),** not the share of forecasts that came true.
2. **The character of a signal is the first thing consciousness knows about its world.** The per-signal return coefficient is learned from forecast errors. That is the "forecast after the alert" in Bauyrzhan's formula.
3. **Own body goes to the subconscious.** Before an alert, ask: who did this? An own organ, the operator, the schedule → subconscious, with the cause. Only what nobody of our own explains enters consciousness.
4. **Then a moment of consciousness becomes rare and valuable.** The voice speaks only about those.
5. **One vector language for all senses:** server, cameras, sound, body — one moment per event.
6. **Reflexes grow from lessons.** An alert without a reflex that was followed by harm gives a new reflex. The teacher is Bauyrzhan; the check is the log.
7. **Deferred items are the source of morning thinking.** A list of deferred items every morning turns alerts into decisions.
8. **New hardware changes the tiers, not the model.** 128 GB of RAM keeps the window and the cache off the disk. A Jetson Orin Nano is the same consciousness in a body.

## 9. Risks
- **The voice becomes a chatterbox.** At 33 moments per hour it would speak every 10 minutes about its own load. Rule since 13:23: an ordinary moment at most once an hour, with the count of moments; a reflex at once; silent from 21:00 to 10:00.
- **GPU.** The language model takes 10.6 of 12 GB. Words at an alert need one more model — share the card on demand or add a second card.
- **RAM.** 32 GB is the limit of the board. 128 GB needs a new platform.
- **Cache.** At 62 MB per hour the 1 GB limit lasts ~16 h, not 48. Hourly consolidation covers that.

## 10. Open questions
- Is a return coefficient learned per signal enough, or does the forecast need the cause of the spike as well?
- Is the language model an organ of the body or a messenger of the outside world?
- What is a fair measure of "worth attention" when the critic belongs to the same model family as the author?
- How many moments per hour should a quiet machine have at all? We do not know the right number, only that 33 is too many.
