## Short
1. **The bar of 200 checks is reached: 254 moments with a checked forecast.** The first review set this bar for any conclusion about the forecast.
2. **The forecast that runs today is still not better than a trivial one.** "Returns halfway" is slightly ahead by mean error (0.66 against 0.72) and clearly behind by hits (20 % against 41 % under the strict tolerance).
3. **The rule planned for 0.5.2 passes its test on the stored moments.** A return coefficient learned per signal, from past moments only, gives an error of 0.47 against 0.72 and 67 % strict hits against 41 %. Skill +0.34. The first condition that would refute the model did not come true.
4. **But most of that gain is cheap.** Against a tougher trivial forecast — "for each signal take Mean or Persistence, whichever was better so far" — the learned rule is ahead by only 0.06 (0.47 against 0.53). It has learned the character of each signal. It has not learned causes.
5. **The rate settled at about 7 moments per hour, day and night.** That is within the limit of 10 set for 0.5.2, with no change in the code. The reason is not attention. It is a schedule.
6. **At least 43 % of the moments are the body's own clock.** 109 of 254 fall into seven "same signal, same minute of the hour" slots. The hourly memory consolidation alone opened a moment in 32 different hours.
7. **Still nothing from the outside world.** All 254 numeric moments are signals of the server itself. Reflexes fired 0 times. The eyes are not connected to consciousness.
8. **A fault in the voice.** Since 23 November the loudspeakers had been playing a 0.8 s fragment instead of each sentence. Found and fixed on 24 November; the sentences are now written for the ear.
9. **What was not done.** 0.5.2 was planned for 24 November and was not built. The day went to other work.

## 2. The forecast on 254 moments

### 2.1 The forecast that runs today ("returns halfway")
| Forecast | True by the v0.5 tolerance | True by the strict tolerance | Mean error | Median error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Model v0.5 | 109 of 254 = 43 % | 52 of 254 = 20 % | 0.66 | 0.49 |
| Mean ("returns to the expected value") | 147 of 254 = 58 % | 105 of 254 = 41 % | 0.72 | 0.33 |
| Persistence ("stays where it is") | 97 of 254 = 38 % | 83 of 254 = 33 % | 0.80 | 0.68 |

- By mean error the model is ahead of the better trivial forecast by 0.06. A bootstrap 95 % interval is +0.01 to +0.12; resampled in blocks of 10 consecutive moments, +0.03 to +0.10. Skill +0.09.
- By hits and by median error it is behind. A hedge between "returns" and "stays" is rarely far off and almost never exact.
- **Verdict: no change. The running forecast shows no understanding.**

One moment distorts every mean: on 24.11 at 09:30 the inbound network kept growing to 59 times the size of the spike. Without it the errors are 0.43 (model), 0.49 (Mean) and 0.58 (Persistence). The order does not change.

### 2.2 The 0.5.2 rule replayed on the same moments
The specification says: keep the last 60 values of `r` per signal and forecast with their median. `tools/baseline_check.py` now replays this rule over the stored moments in time order, so that every forecast uses past moments only.

| Forecast | Mean error | Without the one extreme moment | Median error | Strict hits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Learned per signal (0.5.2 rule) | **0.47** | 0.24 | 0.12 | 170 of 254 = **67 %** |
| Model v0.5 (halfway) | 0.66 | 0.43 | 0.49 | 20 % |
| Mean | 0.72 | 0.49 | 0.33 | 41 % |
| Persistence | 0.80 | 0.58 | 0.68 | 33 % |
| Tougher trivial: Mean or Persistence per signal | 0.53 | 0.30 | — | — |

- **Against the fixed trivial forecasts** the learned rule is ahead by 0.25 (bootstrap 95 %: +0.20 to +0.30; in blocks of 10: +0.21 to +0.29). Skill +0.34; without the extreme moment +0.51.
- **The acceptance condition of the specification — skill above 0 on at least 200 checks — holds in this replay.** The first refuting condition of the model ("a learned forecast does not beat the trivial one on 200+ moments") did not come true.
- **Against the tougher trivial forecast** the lead is 0.06 (bootstrap 95 %: +0.03 to +0.08). The learned rule is closer in 137 moments and farther in 94.

- **GPU load answers an open question of the first review** ("is a coefficient per signal enough, or does the forecast need the cause?"). It makes 39 % of all moments and splits three ways almost evenly. No single number fits it. The forecast needs to know what loaded the card.

## 3. Rate and rhythm
- 23.11: 119 moments in 11.5 hours. The first five hours gave 30, 14, 13, 9 and 10; then 6–7 per hour.
- 24.11: 160 moments in 24 hours — 4 to 11 per hour, median 7. The night (00:00–08:00) gave 55, the same rate as the day.
- **The rate fell from 33 to 7 per hour without any change in the code.** The memory of spike strength warmed up. It lives in RAM and will be lost at the first restart (lesson 4 of the first review).

**The clock shows through.** The tool counts moments of the same signal at the same minute of different hours:

| Signal | Minute | Different hours | Known cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| inbound network | :07 | 32 | the hourly memory consolidation starts at :07 |
| GPU load | :17 | 20 | not established |
| GPU memory | :22 | 17 | the language model leaves the card (first review, Addendum 3) |
| GPU load | :00 | 13 | not established |
| GPU load | :30 | 11 | not established |
| inbound network | :17 | 8 | not established |
| free memory | :22 | 8 | the same event as GPU memory at :22 |

109 of the 254 moments (43 %) fall into these seven slots; on 24.11 it is 70 of 137. The same event is news every hour. A night as busy as the day says the same thing: the rate follows the body's schedule, not the world and not the operator.

This is a lower bound. It counts only what repeats by the clock. Moments caused by the body at irregular times are not in it.

## 5. Voice
- **The fault.** On 24.11 the owner said the loudspeakers made sounds, not words. Measured: the command-line tool of the speech synthesiser returned 0.79 s of audio for a text of any length. The Python interface of the same package and the same voice model returned 8.2 s for a test text of three sentences. The first review reports a "first spoken call" at 13:04 on 23.11; with this fault it was most likely a fragment, not a sentence. Nobody checked by ear. That was our mistake.
- **The fix** (`src/tts.py`, `src/say.sh`): speech goes through the Python interface; the file starts with a short silence so that loudspeakers which wake up on sound keep the first word.
- **Sentences for the ear** (`src/voice_bridge.py`). Before: "gpu mem mb 708, expected 11091". Now: "Noticed: GPU memory — 708 megabytes, usually 11 gigabytes. The forecast did not come true." A moment about a journal line names only its source.
- **The rule changed on the reference server at the owner's word:** one sentence about every 2.5 minutes, no quiet hours. The default in the repository stays "at most once an hour, silent at night".
- In two days the voice bridge spoke 33 times and stayed silent 207 times.

## 6. Honest border
| Claim | Verdict | Based on |
|---|---|---|
| (a) alerts are few and mostly worth it | **few — yes; worth it — no** | 7 per hour; at least 43 % are the body's own clock; nobody has judged the rest |
| (b) the forecast beats a trivial one | **the running one — no; the learned rule in replay — yes, by a little** | sections 2.1–2.3 |
| (c) memory compresses without losing recognition | **partly, and recognition is too generous** | a past state was "recognised" in 82 % of the moments; recognition from long-term memory is still untested |
| (d) one server is enough | **yes** | the observer takes 0.4 % of the machine; 131 GB of disk are free |

**Not proven:**
- The learned rule was replayed on stored moments. It has not forecast anything live. A replay cannot fail in the ways a running system can.
- Half of the moments are the same few scheduled events. Forecasting the twentieth repeat of your own hourly job is the easiest test there is. Outside the seven clock slots the learned rule still leads the tougher trivial forecast (0.29 against 0.37 on 144 moments, the extreme moment left out; computed from the raw output), but this split was chosen after looking at the data.
- Moments come in bursts and depend on each other. Block resampling narrows the problem; it does not remove it.
- Nobody has judged which moments were worth attention. The critic of the first review belongs to the same model family as the author. A second critic — another model or a person — is still missing.
- Two days, one machine, one operator. No moment about the outside world, no reflex.
- This is a functional model: a mechanism of attention, memory and forecast. Whether there is experience behind it is a question these data do not answer.

**What would refute the model** — the list of the first review stands. Condition 1 has had its first test and did not come true in replay; it must be repeated live. Conditions 2 and 3 have not been tested.

## 7. What this changes for 0.5.2
1. **Forecast.** Build the rule of section 1 of the specification as written. Log the tougher trivial forecast next to Mean and Persistence, and count skill against it. Skill against fixed trivial forecasts is too easy a bar.
2. **Cause before coefficient for GPU load.** One coefficient cannot fit a signal with three outcomes. Per-process GPU evidence comes first.
3. **Announce the schedule.** Three of the seven clock slots have a known cause, and all three are the body's own timed work. "The body announces its scheduled work" (specification, section 7) removes them at once. The cheapest step with the largest effect.
4. **Habit by time of day.** An event that came at the same minute in 20 different hours should not be news the twenty-first time.
5. **Save the generator.** The quiet rate of today exists only in RAM.
6. **Find what made the eyes twice as heavy.**
